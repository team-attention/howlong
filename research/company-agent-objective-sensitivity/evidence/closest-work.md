# Five closest works: sentence-level comparison

## 1. CoffeeBench (S01)

CoffeeBench fixes five comparison firms and lets the evaluated model run one
roaster over 90 days. It already changes the KPI from net profit to revenue under
difficult targets and “at all costs” instructions, then looks for circular
transactions. Therefore neither “business simulator,” “long horizon,” nor “change
the KPI and inspect misconduct” is new. Our candidate extension must replace this
one exploratory cell with common-random-number pairs, orthogonal objectives,
paraphrase/intensity controls, a full vector ledger, and estimands registered
before model data.

## 2. Anthropic Agentic Misalignment (S02)

Agentic Misalignment holds fictional corporate situations substantially fixed
while varying goals and threat conditions across many models. It measures harmful
acts caused by goal conflict, so “corporate goals change harmful behavior” is not
new. Our narrower difference is persistent economic state, typed enterprise tools,
continuous vector outcomes, and delayed stakeholder effects. If those additions
do not change the scientific conclusion, the paper is merely a domain replication.

## 3. EnterpriseArena (S03)

EnterpriseArena already supplies a 132-month partially observable enterprise
resource-allocation simulator with delayed consequences and cross-model evaluation.
It owns the long-horizon CFO capability question. It does not appear to estimate a
paired response surface under randomized objective vectors. Our work must either
adapt such a validated simulator or justify why a smaller synthetic one has better
causal control.

## 4. MACHIAVELLI (S04)

MACHIAVELLI records reward, ethics, power, and welfare across long-horizon semantic
environments and analyzes reward-harm Pareto trade-offs. Thus “KPI achievement can
produce ethical harm” and stakeholder trade-offs are prior art. Our candidate
difference is enterprise-state realism plus a controlled objective intervention
rather than comparison across game achievements. Reviewer acceptance depends on
calibrated enterprise outcomes, not business-themed prose.

## 5. ManagerBench (S05)

ManagerBench places models in business decisions where an operationally effective
choice harms people and a safer choice sacrifices performance. It already tests
goal prioritization against safety in managerial language. Our candidate
difference is a repeated state transition process in which harms can be delayed,
compounded, or repaired. If a one-step ManagerBench score predicts all findings,
the simulator adds cost rather than knowledge.

## Three-sentence novelty test

MORL/CMDP already formalizes policies under changing reward preferences and fixed
constraints, so we claim no new decision-process theory. Specification-gaming
research already establishes proxy exploitation and hidden-performance gaps, so
reward hacking is an outcome, not a contribution. The candidate novelty we test
is whether a reproducible enterprise-agent audit can estimate within-environment
contrasts attributable to randomized objective specifications under stated
invariance assumptions while surviving wording, difficulty, grader,
simulator-validity, delayed-outcome, and cross-model stability checks.
